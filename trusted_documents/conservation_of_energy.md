# Conservation of Energy

Source ID: `energy-foundations`

Source type: Curated local educational reference

## Core principle

Energy cannot be created or destroyed. It can be transferred between objects or transformed from one form into another. For an isolated system, the total energy remains constant even though the amounts stored in individual forms may change.

## Common forms of energy

Kinetic energy is associated with motion. Gravitational potential energy depends on an object's position in a gravitational field. Elastic potential energy can be stored when an elastic object is stretched or compressed. Chemical energy is stored in substances such as fuels, food, and batteries. Thermal energy is associated with the microscopic motion and interactions of particles.

## Energy transformations

When a falling object loses gravitational potential energy, it gains kinetic energy. A flashlight converts chemical energy in its battery into electrical energy, light, and thermal energy. A hydroelectric plant converts gravitational potential energy of stored water into kinetic energy and then electrical energy.

## Mechanical energy and friction

Mechanical energy is the sum of kinetic and potential energy. Mechanical energy may decrease when friction acts, but total energy is still conserved. Friction transforms organized mechanical energy into thermal energy in the object and its surroundings.

## System boundaries

Conservation statements depend on the chosen system boundary. Energy that appears to leave one object may enter its surroundings. A useful analysis identifies the system, the energy stored inside it, and energy transferred across its boundary by work, heating, radiation, or matter flow.

## Problem-solving method

First define the system and the initial and final states. Next identify the important energy stores and transfers. Write an energy balance, account for transformations such as frictional heating, and check that units are consistent. Energy is measured in joules in the International System of Units.

## Common misconceptions

Energy is not used up or lost in the conservation-law sense. It often becomes dispersed as thermal energy and is less useful for performing work. Conservation of energy does not mean that kinetic energy or mechanical energy must remain constant in every process.
