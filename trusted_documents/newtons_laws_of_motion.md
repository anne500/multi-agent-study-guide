# Newton's Laws of Motion

Source ID: `newton-motion-foundations`

Source type: Curated local educational reference

## First law

An object remains at rest or continues moving with constant velocity unless a net external force acts on it. The first law describes inertia, which is an object's resistance to a change in velocity. Balanced forces produce zero net force and therefore no acceleration.

## Second law

The acceleration of an object is determined by the net force and the object's mass. For constant mass, the relationship is written as `F_net = m a`. Acceleration points in the direction of the net force. Force is measured in newtons, where one newton equals one kilogram meter per second squared.

## Third law

When object A exerts a force on object B, object B exerts an equal-magnitude force in the opposite direction on object A. The two forces act on different objects, so they do not cancel each other on a free-body diagram for one object.

## Net force and free-body diagrams

A free-body diagram represents one selected object and the external forces acting on it. Common forces include weight, normal force, tension, friction, drag, and applied forces. Vector addition of all external forces gives the net force.

## Mass and weight

Mass measures inertia and is expressed in kilograms. Weight is the gravitational force on an object and is written near Earth's surface as `W = m g`. Mass remains the same when location changes, while weight can change when gravitational field strength changes.

## Everyday applications

A passenger moves forward when a vehicle stops suddenly because the passenger's body tends to maintain its velocity. A larger net force produces a larger acceleration for the same mass. Walking is possible because a foot pushes backward on the ground while the ground pushes forward on the person.

## Common misconceptions

Motion does not require a continuing net force when velocity is constant. Action and reaction forces do not act on the same object. A heavier object does not necessarily fall faster than a lighter one when air resistance is negligible, because both have the same gravitational acceleration.
